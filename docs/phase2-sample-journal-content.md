# Phase 2 Manual Testing - Sample Journal Entry & Notes

## Sample Journal Entry

Copy and paste this into today's journal entry, then highlight the text in [square brackets] to create notes:

---

Today I've been reflecting on what really matters to me and where I want to go in life.

[I realize that authenticity is incredibly important to me - being true to myself even when it's uncomfortable or unpopular.] I've spent too much time trying to fit into others' expectations, and it's exhausting. Moving forward, I want to make decisions that align with who I really am, not who others think I should be.

[I believe that meaningful relationships are built on vulnerability and honest communication.] When I look back at my closest friendships, they all started with moments where someone took a risk and shared something real. Surface-level conversations feel hollow now. I want to cultivate deeper connections by showing up as my authentic self.

I've been thinking about my career path lately. [My goal is to build work that has a positive impact on people's mental health and well-being.] I'm tired of feeling like my work is just a paycheck. I want to wake up excited about what I'm creating and know that it's helping people live better lives. Even if it means taking a pay cut or starting over, I think it's worth it.

On a more practical level, [I'm committed to establishing a daily meditation practice for at least 20 minutes.] I've tried this before and failed, but I know it helps me stay grounded and present. This time I'm going to set a specific time (7am before work) and treat it like a non-negotiable appointment with myself.

[I've noticed that I tend to avoid conflict, even when speaking up would lead to better outcomes.] This pattern shows up everywhere - in my relationships, at work, even with my family. I end up resentful because I don't advocate for myself. I need to practice stating my needs clearly and respectfully, even when it feels uncomfortable. It's not about being aggressive; it's about respecting myself enough to have a voice.

---

## Content for the 5 Notes

### Note 1: Authenticity (Disguised)
**Highlight:** "I realize that authenticity is incredibly important to me - being true to myself even when it's uncomfortable or unpopular."

**Note Content to Add:**
```
Had that meeting with Sarah's team today. Everyone was nodding along with the new direction, but I could feel this knot in my stomach. I know the approach won't work - I've seen it fail twice before at other companies. But I just... sat there. Smiled. Nodded.

Drove home thinking about my dad. He spent 30 years at that insurance company, coming home exhausted every day, complaining about the same things but never changing anything. "That's just how it is," he'd say. I swore I wouldn't be like that.

Yet here I am, wearing the mask. Saying what people want to hear. It's like I'm performing this character of who I think I should be, and somewhere along the way I lost track of the actual me underneath.

The weird thing is, the times I've felt most alive recently were when I screwed up and accidentally said what I really thought. Like when I told Marcus his presentation was confusing - he was annoyed at first, but then we had this real conversation and actually fixed the problems. Or when I admitted to Jamie that I had no idea what I was doing with the new codebase. She laughed and said "Thank god, I thought I was the only one."

Maybe those weren't screw-ups. Maybe that was just me.
```

---

### Note 2: Vulnerability (Disguised)
**Highlight:** "I believe that meaningful relationships are built on vulnerability and honest communication."

**Note Content to Add:**
```
Thinking about last Saturday at Chris and Alex's place. We were all drinking wine, telling stories, and somehow the conversation shifted. Alex started talking about their depression last year - not in a dramatic way, just matter-of-fact. "Yeah, I couldn't get out of bed for like two weeks. Couldn't even text people back."

And then everyone just... opened up. Maya talked about her divorce. Chris talked about losing his mom. I shared that thing about my panic attacks that I've never told anyone except my therapist.

That was three hours of conversation and it felt like 20 minutes. Contrast that with the happy hour we did with the work crew last month - two hours of talking about nothing, and I was checking my watch every ten minutes.

I keep thinking about what made Saturday different. Nobody was trying to impress anyone. Nobody was performing. Just actual humans admitting they don't have it all figured out.

The next day Alex texted me: "Hey, thanks for sharing that stuff last night. Made me feel less alone."

I realized I'd been feeling less alone too. Like I'd been carrying around all this stuff in silence, thinking everyone else had their shit together. Turns out we're all just stumbling through.

Why don't we do that more often?
```

### Note 3: Meaningful Work (Disguised)
**Highlight:** "My goal is to build work that has a positive impact on people's mental health and well-being."

**Note Content to Add:**
```
Got the bonus notification today. Should feel good, right? It's a decent chunk of money. But I just stared at my screen feeling... empty.

I spent the entire quarter optimizing ad click-through rates. Made the company an extra $2M in revenue. And for what? So people click on more ads? So shareholders get richer?

Closed my laptop and went for a walk. Ended up at that coffee shop where the barista - I think her name is Maya - always remembers my order. She asked how my day was and I almost started crying. Had to pretend I was checking my phone.

When I got back I opened up that old project folder from two years ago. The mental health app prototype. I was so excited about it - this tool that could actually help people notice patterns in their mood, connect with support when they needed it. I showed it to three people and they all said the same thing: "Cool, but how does it make money?"

So I shelved it. Took the "practical" job. The one with benefits and stock options and a path to senior engineer.

But here's the thing: I can't remember the last time I was excited to start my workday. I used to wake up with ideas, couldn't wait to build things. Now I wake up and think about how many meetings I have to sit through.

My therapist asked me last week: "If money wasn't a concern, what would you be doing?" I couldn't even answer. That's sad.

Actually, I could answer. I just didn't want to say it out loud because it felt naive: I'd be building things that help people feel less alone. Less anxious. More okay with being human.
```

### Note 4: Meditation Practice (Disguised)
**Highlight:** "I'm committed to establishing a daily meditation practice for at least 20 minutes."

**Note Content to Add:**
```
Week three of trying this morning sitting thing. Set my alarm for 6:45 again today. Hit snooze twice, obviously.

When I finally got up and sat on the cushion, my brain was already spinning: emails I need to send, that awkward thing I said to Jordan yesterday, whether I should replace my laptop, did I pay the electricity bill, why does my knee hurt, maybe I should get new running shoes...

20 minutes feels SO LONG when you're just sitting there watching your mind be chaotic.

But then something weird happened around minute 15. My breathing just kind of... settled. And for maybe 90 seconds I wasn't thinking about anything. Just breathing. Existing.

The alarm went off and I felt disappointed. Wanted to keep sitting.

Carried that feeling for maybe an hour. Made breakfast without scrolling Twitter. Actually tasted the coffee. Noticed the sunlight on the counter.

By 9am I was back to my usual scattered mess, but still. Those 90 seconds were something.

I keep reading about people who've been meditating for years, talking about equanimity and presence. I don't know if I believe in that stuff. But I do know that on the days I sit, even when it feels like nothing is happening, I'm slightly less of a stressed-out disaster.

Also noticed I'm less likely to snap at people when they interrupt me. And I don't doom-scroll as much before bed. Could be correlation, not causation, but... maybe not.

Setting the alarm for 6:45 tomorrow. We'll see if I actually get up.
```

---

### Note 5: Conflict Avoidance (Disguised)
**Highlight:** "I've noticed that I tend to avoid conflict, even when speaking up would lead to better outcomes."

**Note Content to Add:**
```
Mom called tonight. She wants to visit next month and stay with me for a week. A WEEK.

I love my mom, but she has this way of taking over my entire life when she visits. Rearranges my kitchen. Makes comments about my apartment being messy. Asks when I'm going to settle down and find someone serious. Shows up to my place at 8am expecting me to have plans for the day.

I wanted to say: "Mom, I love you, but a week is too long. How about a long weekend instead? And maybe you could stay at a hotel this time so we both have some space?"

What I actually said: "Yeah, that sounds great! Can't wait."

And now I'm sitting here with that familiar pit in my stomach. The one that says: you just agreed to something you don't want to do, and now you're going to spend the next month dreading it, and then the actual week being quietly resentful, and your mom won't even know why you seem distant.

This is the same pattern. Always the same pattern.

Roommate situation in college - lived with those guys for two years, never once told them their dishes rotting in the sink bothered me. Just silently cleaned up after them and hated them for it.

Last relationship - he wanted to move in together, I wasn't ready, I said yes anyway. Spent six months feeling suffocated. When I finally broke up with him, he was blindsided. "Why didn't you tell me you were unhappy?"

Current job - agreed to take on the Johnson account even though I'm already underwater. Boss assumes I'm fine. I'm not fine.

At some point I convinced myself that keeping the peace was the same as being kind. But I'm starting to think it's actually just cowardice dressed up as niceness.

And the worst part is, the peace I'm keeping is fake anyway. Nobody is actually getting what they need - not them, and definitely not me.
```
